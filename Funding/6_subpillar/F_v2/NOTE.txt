v2 changes from v1, based on manual review of F_v1_claude-sonnet-4-6_results.xlsx (18 of 52 rows
exact_match=False).

Of the 18 mismatches: 16 were the LLM being correct (ground truth wrong) per the user's own review
notes — no prompt change needed for those. 2 were "out of scope" grants (a PHA-bioplastic
biomanufacturing grant, a lignin-to-chemicals valorization grant) that aren't really alternative-
protein food grants at all but got forced into a BF/PF answer anyway by the "every Fermentation
grant must resolve to something" rule — a data-scoping observation, not a prompt fix, left as-is
for now (flagging here in case it's worth revisiting whether these should be excluded from the
Fermentation-pillar sample entirely, upstream of this prompt).

The one real, recurring mistake (2 rows: "MYCOFACT" id 12, "Synthetically engineered microalgae for
improved gut function" id 39): the LLM classified strain-engineering-for-overexpression grants as
PF whenever a specific compound (an enzyme/metabolite in MYCOFACT; Vitamin B12/Lutein/
exopolysaccharides in the microalgae grant) was named as the target of the engineering — even
though in both cases the compound stays within the organism's biomass, which remains the whole
food ingredient. v1's decision rule said "strain development... whose stated purpose is producing
or overexpressing a specific target molecule... → PF", with no requirement that the molecule
actually be separated from the cells — that was the bug.

Fix: the rule now explicitly requires extraction/purification/isolation away from the cells before
something counts as PF. Overexpressing or enriching a named compound WITHIN the biomass, where the
biomass itself (containing that compound) is still the food product, stays BF regardless of how
targeted the engineering is or how much the grant frames that one compound as its central
innovation. PF is reserved for when the specific molecule is isolated out and used as a standalone
ingredient separate from the biomass. Both the decision rule and the BF category definition were
updated to state this explicitly (the BF definition now says as much: "Includes biomass that has
been strain-engineered to enrich a specific nutrient or compound within it, as long as that
compound is not subsequently isolated away from the biomass").

Also, per the user's request: category names changed from the full "Biomass fermentation"/
"Precision fermentation" to the short codes "BF"/"PF" throughout (matching the ground truth's own
vocabulary directly, and matching the original patents prompt's "BF (Biomass fermentation):" /
"PF (Precision fermentation):" definition style). The notebook's PILLAR_CATS["F"] and
collapse_fermentation function were updated to match — collapse_fermentation no longer needs to
translate full category names, since the schema fields are now literally "BF"/"PF" already.

Added a third boolean category, "Unclear" (also decided during the same review pass, before any
run of v2): row id 5 ("GoCreate") had no organism/product named in the abstract at all — just a
vague reference to a "Fermtech" company — so v1's ambiguous-fallback rule ("default to flagging
both BF and PF true") kicked in, predicting Mixed. Ground truth was actually BF (the user happens
to know the company does biomass fermentation, information not present in the text). The user
flagged this as a case that should probably route to manual review rather than get a guessed
label at all.

Checked how often this specific fallback (as opposed to a real multi-modal-institute Mixed case)
actually fired in the v1 run: only 1 of 52 rows (the GoCreate row itself) — the other two Mixed
predictions (Porphyridium purpureum id 16, the bioprocess-monitoring platform id 27) were genuine
dual-route/institute-scale grants the user agreed with. So conflating "genuinely can't tell" with
"explicitly does both" under one Mixed label was hiding two very different situations behind one
value, and Mixed guesses for the former case will score as wrong against a single-label BF/PF
ground truth anyway — there's no accuracy cost to admitting uncertainty instead. This mirrors the
original patents prompt's own NA category and its v1 notes' own conclusion for the equivalent case
("not fixable by prompt wording... flagging in case family-level context becomes available").

**"Unclear" was tried and then reverted after the first real run.** Added as a third boolean
(flagged true, with BF and PF both left false, only when the text genuinely doesn't specify a
route — distinguished from the broad-institute/platform rule, which still flags both BF and PF
true when a grant explicitly describes both modalities), with rows routed to manual review
(excluded from scoring) instead of guessed. Based on the v1 review this was expected to fire on
roughly 1/52 rows.

Actual v2 run: 13 of 52 rows came back Unclear — the model was applying it far more liberally than
"no organism/product/activity named at all" (the intended bar), closer to "any hesitation about
which route." That's an unacceptable amount of manual-review overhead for what it bought. Per the
user's decision, Unclear was removed entirely and the original v1 ambiguous-fallback rule restored
("every grant must resolve to at least one of BF or PF; default to flagging both true when
genuinely unclear") — accepting that Mixed will occasionally be wrong in the rare case where the
text truly gives no signal (as with the GoCreate row that prompted this whole detour), rather than
carrying a 25%-of-the-sample manual-review burden to avoid it. The notebook's needs_review-column/
excluded-from-metrics machinery added to support Unclear was reverted along with it.

Everything else (the whey/casein/egg/collagen/heme canonical-PF-molecule rule, the "don't decide
from organism alone" rule, the broad-institute/platform Mixed rule, the BF/PF extraction-boundary
fix) is unchanged and stays in v2.
