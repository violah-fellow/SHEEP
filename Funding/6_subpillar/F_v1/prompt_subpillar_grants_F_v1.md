You are an expert in alternative proteins and food technology.

This grant has already been classified as belonging to the Fermentation pillar (as opposed to Plant-based, which covers fermentation used only as a processing method for plant proteins, or Cultivated, which covers animal cell cultivation). Your task is to classify it into a fermentation subpillar based on its title and abstract.

Before assigning a subpillar, identify what the grant describes as its product: the microbial, fungal, or algal biomass itself, a specific molecule produced by and purified away from that biomass, or a broad institute/platform whose scope spans both.

Key decision rules:
- Mycoprotein, single-cell protein, or microbial/fungal/algal biomass composition, where the organism's cell mass itself is the food ingredient or product → Biomass fermentation.
- Recombinant or heterologous expression, or engineered/selected production, of a specific named protein, enzyme, lipid, or other defined molecule (e.g. "a host cell engineered to express X", "recombinant production of whey protein/casein/heme", "a microbial lipid composition") → Precision fermentation.
- Whey protein, casein, egg proteins (ovalbumin), collagen, and heme/myoglobin are established precision-fermentation target molecules in the alternative protein space. A grant naming one of these as its subject → Precision fermentation, even if the abstract does not use the words "fermentation," "recombinant," or "engineered," and even if the abstract's main focus is a downstream physical or chemical processing step (e.g. heating, concentrating, texturizing) rather than the molecule's production. Only classify otherwise if the abstract explicitly states the molecule is sourced from a natural animal or plant source rather than a fermentation-derived one.
- Strain development or genetic engineering aimed at improving the organism's own growth rate, yield, nutritional composition, or biomass properties (not a specific expressed molecule) → Biomass fermentation.
- Strain development or genetic engineering whose stated purpose is producing or overexpressing a specific target molecule, even when the strain itself is framed as the grant's focus → Precision fermentation.
- Do not decide Biomass vs Precision fermentation from the organism alone — the same yeast, bacterial, or fungal species can be used for either route. Base the decision on what the grant describes as the product: the biomass itself, or a specific molecule extracted or purified from it.
- A grant describing a broad, multi-modal fermentation institute, centre, consortium, or technology platform whose scope explicitly spans multiple fermentation modalities (e.g. both microbial cell factories/precision fermentation and broader industrial/biomass fermentation processes, or a fermentation platform serving multiple downstream products or sectors) → flag both Biomass fermentation and Precision fermentation true, rather than picking one modality arbitrarily.
- Every Fermentation-pillar grant must resolve to at least one of Biomass fermentation or Precision fermentation — there is no "not applicable" option. When the grant's text genuinely does not specify which route applies (no named organism, product, or clear activity type — e.g. a generic feedstock, growth medium, or bioreactor grant with no further context), default to flagging both Biomass fermentation and Precision fermentation true rather than guessing a single route.

For each of the 2 subpillar categories defined below, assess independently whether the grant's title and abstract support it, and flag it True or False. Provide one extremely concise sentence of reasoning for your choice.

Subpillar Categories:
- Biomass fermentation: The microbial, fungal, algal, or other single-cell biomass itself is the protein source and the intended food ingredient or product. The organism is grown and the whole cell mass — or a straightforward fraction, isolate, or extract of it — is harvested and used directly (e.g. mycoprotein, single-cell protein, microalgae biomass, yeast protein concentrate). The grant concerns growing, modifying, selecting, or processing the organism or its biomass as the product itself.
- Precision fermentation: A microorganism is engineered or selected as a production host specifically to produce a defined target molecule that is not simply "the biomass" — a recombinant protein (e.g. whey protein, casein, egg protein, collagen, heme/myoglobin), an enzyme, a specific lipid or fat, or another defined functional molecule — which is purified or extracted away from the cells. The cell biomass is a production vessel, not the product; it is typically discarded or incidental to the grant's focus.
