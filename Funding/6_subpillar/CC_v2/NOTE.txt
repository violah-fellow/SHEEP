v2 is a full redesign, not an iteration on v1 (CellAg/All/Agnostic). v1 was built from the legacy
"Sub-production pillar" values, which only existed on 32 of 117 Cross-cutting rows — the user ran
v1, found the categories "a bit useless," and realised they'd never actually looked at how sparse
and seemingly arbitrary those legacy labels were until seeing the results. v2 was built by reading
the title/abstract (plus Award purpose/Research category for cross-referencing) of ALL 117
Cross-cutting rows from scratch, not just the 32 with an old label.

Natural clusters found in the full 117: broad multi-pillar R&D (45 rows), hub/network/training/
consortium-building (20), generic narrow technical studies (15), consumer/market/socioeconomic
research (14), novel/other-platform or mistagged rows (10), cross-platform hybrid technique (6),
sustainability/environmental-impact assessment (5), policy/macro catch-all (2).

Design decisions, all confirmed with the user:

1. **CellAg + All merge into "Broad R&D."** Read at face value, CellAg and All were never two
   different grant types — they were two different framings (cell-ag-specific language vs.
   whole-sector language) of the same thing: broad, multi-pillar technical research. Once the 92
   previously-unlabelled rows are read too, the same content pattern holds at ~4x the scale (45
   Broad R&D rows vs. the old 25-row combined CellAg+All bucket).

2. **"Technical research" kept as a separate category from "Broad R&D," not merged.** Both are
   technical/scientific research in nature — the difference is scope (spans multiple platforms vs.
   a narrow single-lab study). Considered merging since "type of research" was the user's stated
   organising principle, but the user chose to keep breadth as its own distinction.

3. **"Socioeconomic" merges consumer/market/policy research with sustainability/environmental-
   impact-assessment (LCA/footprint-tool/systems-modelling) research.** These read in a noticeably
   different register (survey/attitude/qualitative work vs. quantitative modelling), but both are
   clearly non-technical and society/systems-facing, and the user chose the simpler merged version
   over keeping the LCA-style cluster as its own third-of-four category.

4. **"Agnostic" dropped entirely.** Only 2 of 117 rows genuinely fit "not tied to any specific
   project/technology/method" even after reading everything — and one of the two legacy-tagged
   "Agnostic" rows ("Center for Sustainable Protein Launches at Imperial") doesn't hold up on close
   reading anyway; it's a research-centre launch, i.e. Broad R&D/hub content, not a pure macro item.

5. **"Hub/Network/Training/Consortium-building" is NOT a category, despite being a real 20-row
   cluster.** Per the user's explicit framing: subpillar should track the type of research being
   funded, not the funding mechanism — that's already Award Purpose's job (Research and
   development vs. Education and training vs. Networking vs. Equipment and infrastructure vs.
   Research infrastructure). Confirmed via cross-tab that Award Purpose already captures this
   cluster almost completely (every row whose Award purpose includes Networking, Equipment and
   infrastructure, or Research infrastructure falls into this cluster or the 2-row Policy cluster).
   So hub-shaped grants (NAPIC, CARMA, ICAN, etc.) are classified here by what they actually
   research instead — e.g. NAPIC funds broad cross-platform R&D, so its subpillar is Broad R&D,
   regardless of it also being a "centre."

6. **Single-label, not multi-label, with Broad R&D as an explicit fallback.** The first real run
   (before this change) used the same TRUE/FALSE-per-category boolean schema as every other
   pillar in this pipeline, and several rows came back multi-true (e.g. "Broad R&D;
   Socioeconomic") — not what was wanted once the taxonomy was designed as one-label-per-grant.
   Rather than keep independent booleans and add a "pick only one"/tie-break instruction (which
   the model could still violate), the schema itself was changed to a single `Literal` field
   constrained to exactly one of the three category names — this enforces "exactly one" at the
   API's structured-output level (Claude is given a tool whose input schema has an `enum`
   constraint, so it cannot return more than one value), not just via a prompt instruction. The
   prompt's decision rules were reordered into a priority list (Socioeconomic, then Technical
   research, then Broad R&D as the catch-all default) instead of three independent true/false
   tests. This required a genuine notebook change alongside the prompt change: a new
   `make_single_label_schema` builder (`Literal[tuple(cats)]` instead of one boolean per
   category), a `SCHEMA_MODE` dict (`"multi_label"` for F/PB, `"single_label"` for CC) selecting
   which builder runs, and `collapse_crosscutting`/`build_predictions` reading the single
   `category` field directly instead of iterating booleans. F and PB are unaffected — they're
   genuinely multi-label (a grant can be BF+PF at once) and keep the original boolean schema.

7. **"Technical work + minor socioeconomic component" stays Broad R&D, not Socioeconomic.** Raised
   as a concern before this ran for real: a broad multi-platform R&D grant that also evaluates
   consumer acceptance as one work package could get pulled toward Socioeconomic just because the
   model spots "consumer acceptance" language, even though the Socioeconomic definition already
   said "no wet-lab or process-engineering content." A qualifier at the end of a bullet is easy to
   skim past, especially once Socioeconomic is checked first in a priority list. Fixed by adding
   an explicit rule ahead of the category-specific ones: any grant with genuine wet-lab/process-
   engineering/product-development content anywhere in its scope stays Broad R&D or Technical
   research, regardless of an accompanying consumer/socioeconomic evaluation component — and
   narrowed the Socioeconomic definition to require that non-technical research be the entire
   activity, not a secondary component alongside real technical work.

8. **Motivational/benefit framing is not itself a socioeconomic research component.** Caught during
   manual review of the first real run: "An innovative whey protein powder that includes
   lactoferrin and NAD+..." (GoFitt/MilaK) — a single, narrow fermented-whey-protein-powder
   product-development project — came back Broad R&D instead of Technical research. The abstract's
   framing (lactose-intolerance health equity, affordability for low-income communities, carbon
   emissions, workforce productivity) is standard grant-writing motivation for why the product
   matters, not a described socioeconomic research task — but rule #7's fix ("technical + a
   socioeconomic component stays Broad R&D/Technical research") didn't distinguish "genuinely
   performs socioeconomic research" from "mentions societal benefits," so the model apparently
   read the benefit-framing language as if it were a real component and bumped scope up to Broad
   R&D. Fixed by adding an explicit distinction: a socioeconomic component only counts when the
   grant explicitly describes DOING that research (e.g. "we will conduct consumer surveys,"
   "assessing market acceptance through interviews") — narrative motivation using health/
   environmental/societal benefit language does not, on its own, elevate a narrow single-product
   development project to Broad R&D. Both the Technical research and Broad R&D definitions were
   updated to state this explicitly.

## Critical: this run has no ground truth to score against

Unlike Fermentation (BF/PF/Mixed) and Plant-Based (TF), these 3 categories don't exist anywhere in
the raw "Sub-production pillar" column — they're invented fresh from reading content, not derived
from an existing label. Comparing predictions against the old CellAg/All/Agnostic/CM-for-PB/
Consumer/blank values would be meaningless (different vocabulary entirely), so the notebook does
NOT compute exact-match/precision/recall/Jaccard for this pillar, and does not balance-sample by
category (there's nothing to balance against) — it runs the LLM across all 117 Cross-cutting rows
and saves title/abstract/predicted categories/reasoning for manual review. This first v2 run *is*
the ground-truth-generation step: once reviewed and corrected, that becomes the basis for scoring
any future v3.
