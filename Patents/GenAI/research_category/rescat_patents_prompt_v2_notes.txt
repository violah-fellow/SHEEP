1. STRAIN DEVELOPMENT OVER-TRIGGERING FOR INHIBITOR SCREENING — PB

Affected cases: Two instances of the same patent about screening procedures to
identify inhibitors of cell envelope proteinases from lactic acid bacteria.
Training label: Food safety & quality. LLM assigned: Strain development.

Root cause: The strong strain development guidance added in v1 caused the LLM to
trigger on any mention of LAB or microbial strains, even when the patent's primary
subject is an inhibitor compound rather than the strain itself.

Fix (PB only):
- Added preamble bullet: "A patent describing a screening method to identify inhibitor
  compounds targeting microbial enzymes, where the inhibitor compound (not a novel
  strain) is the primary subject → Food safety & quality (not Strain development)"
- Added to Strain development definition: "Does not include patents whose primary
  subject is an inhibitor compound, screening assay, or other molecule that targets
  or interacts with microbial enzymes but does not claim a novel strain — these belong
  in Food safety & quality."

2. TARGET MOLECULE SELECTION OVER BIOPROCESS DESIGN — F

Affected cases: Two instances of a casein production patent. Training label: Bioprocess
design. LLM assigned: Target molecule selection, because casein is a named recombinant
target protein.

Root cause: The existing guidance ("when the method is the primary innovation, prefer
Bioprocess design") was not strong enough when a well-known target molecule (casein)
was prominently named.

Fix (F only):
- Strengthened existing preamble bullet to include a concrete casein example and an
  explicit warning: "do not let the presence of a named target molecule override this."

---

3. TEXTURIZATION METHODS OVER END PRODUCT FORMULATION — F

Affected cases: Two patents describing compositions of textured fermentation-derived
products (textured fungal protein pieces; mycoprotein food/feed with alginate cross-
linking). Training label: End product formulation. LLM assigned: Texturization methods.

Root cause: Both patents describe texture as a key product property, causing the
Texturization methods trigger to fire even though the primary claimed subject is the
product formula/composition, not a texturization process or apparatus.

Fix (F only):
- Added to End product formulation definition: "A patent that claims a product
  COMPOSITION — where texture is one property of the claimed formula rather than the
  primary process innovation — belongs here, not in Texturization methods."

---

4. FEEDSTOCKS OVER BIOPROCESS DESIGN — F

Affected case: "PRODUCTION OF A FUNGAL FERMENTATION MEDIUM FROM BREWER'S SPENT GRAIN."
Training label: Bioprocess design. LLM assigned: Feedstocks.

Root cause: The Feedstocks priority rule added in v1 ("assign Feedstocks over Bioprocess
Design when the primary novelty is the input material") was too aggressive for patents
where both a novel feedstock AND a production process are described. The LLM correctly
identified BSG as a novel feedstock but over-applied the rule.

Fix (F only):
- Softened the Feedstocks priority rule: "Assign Feedstocks over Bioprocess Design when
  the primary novelty is the input material ALONE and no significant process design
  innovation is described alongside it — if both a novel feedstock and a production
  process are central to the patent, prefer Bioprocess design."

5. SCAFFOLDING OVER BIOPROCESS DESIGN — CC

Affected case: "CELL CULTURE CONSTRUCT" — a construct comprising fibres with external
surfaces for cell adhesion and culturing, integrated within a bioreactor module.
Training label: Bioprocess design. LLM assigned: Scaffolding.

Root cause: The existing preamble bullet said "scaffold element integrated within a
bioreactor" but the patent uses language like "cell culture construct" and "fibres for
cell adhesion" — the LLM pattern-matched to Scaffolding before processing the bioreactor
module context.

Fix (CC only):
- Made the preamble bullet more concrete: now explicitly mentions "cell culture
  construct, fibrous material for cell adhesion, or scaffold element that is designed
  as part of or integrated within a bioreactor module or production system → Bioprocess
  design (not Scaffolding)."
