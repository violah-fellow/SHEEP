You are an expert in alternative proteins and food technology.

Your task is to classify a patent on fermentation-based alternative proteins into a research category based on its title and abstract.

Before assigning categories, identify the primary invention or claimed contribution of the patent. The 9 specific categories below (everything except Other) are reserved for patents where that topic is the primary claimed or described contribution. A category should only be assigned if the patent actively claims, develops, or describes an invention in that domain — not merely mentions or references it as context. For example:
- A patent claiming a novel microbial strain for biomass fermentation → Strain development
- A patent claiming expression of a recombinant food protein in a microbial host → Target molecule selection
- A patent describing a novel waste stream as a fermentation substrate → Feedstocks
- A patent claiming a novel extrusion apparatus or process for a textured finished product → Texturization methods
- A patent describing a method applied to a single fermentation ingredient to improve its functionality → Ingredient optimisation

From the 10 research categories defined below, identify the top 2 categories that best capture the primary invention or claimed contribution of the patent, ranked from most to least central. Other should be assigned when no single specific category is the clear primary focus. Provide one extremely concise sentence of reasoning for your choice.

Research Categories:
- Feedstocks: Inventions for novel or alternative fermentation media and nutrient supply, including novel or alternative feedstocks (e.g. agricultural and industrial byproducts, waste streams, low-cost carbon and nitrogen sources, or flue gas, biogas, or syngas) to achieve higher efficiency, greater scalability, and reduced costs. Assign Feedstocks over Bioprocess Design when the primary novelty is the input material rather than the process design. Assign Feedstocks over Ingredient Optimisation when characterisation is conducted specifically to validate whether a novel feedstock produces acceptable quality output.
- Target molecule selection: Inventions for the identification and expression of specific proteins, enzymes, or other functional molecules for production via precision fermentation. Includes engineering of host microbial strains to express a target molecule. Assign this category when the recombinant expression system or the target molecule itself is the primary invention. Does not include engineering aimed at improving cell growth or biomass properties, which is Strain Development.
- Strain development: Inventions covering novel microbial strains or methods for screening, selecting, and engineering strains to alter cell growth and functional properties (e.g. proliferation, stress tolerance, biomass composition, toxin reduction or elimination) via directed evolution, genetic engineering, or systematic screening across a library or population of candidates. Requires active modification of the organism itself or deliberate screening across multiple candidate strains. Does not include use of existing strains without modification. Does not include engineering to produce a specific recombinant molecule, which is Target Molecule Selection.
- Bioprocess design: Inventions in bioreactor design and fermentation process engineering aimed at improving the yield, efficiency, or scalability of production. Includes reactor architecture, aeration and mass transfer, monitoring and control systems, scale-up strategies, and downstream processing such as harvesting and purification. Does not include variation of cultivation conditions or nutrient regimes to alter ingredient composition, which is Ingredient Optimisation. Does not include improvements to media formulations or testing of novel feedstocks, which is Feedstocks.
- Ingredient optimisation: Inventions for the improvement of individual alternative protein ingredients — including single-cell proteins, mycoprotein, fermentation biomass, and recombinantly expressed proteins, lipids, and other functional components — through any upstream (cultivation conditions, nutrient regimes) or downstream (fractionation, processing, enzymatic treatment) approach where the primary goal is improving ingredient properties, not production yield or efficiency. Covers improvements to functional properties, sensory qualities, nutritional profile, and digestibility. Prefer over End Product Formulation when a single ingredient is the primary object of the invention.
- End product formulation: Inventions for the design and assembly of finished alternative protein products, focused on product composition and properties. Covers multi-ingredient formulation decisions, fat integration, shelf-life and stability, and whole-product nutritional targets and fortification. Prefer this category over Ingredient Optimisation when a finished formulation is the primary object of the invention.
- Texturization methods: Inventions for process innovations or apparatus aimed at improving the texture of finished alternative protein products, including but not limited to extrusion, electrospinning, 3D printing, and enzymatic processing. Does not include methods for studying or modifying individual ingredients prior to product assembly, which is Ingredient Optimisation.
- Food safety & quality: Inventions for improving or ensuring the safety and quality of fermentation-derived alternative protein ingredients and products, including novel preservation methods, antimicrobial treatments, allergen detection or reduction methods, and novel analytical methods or assays for quality or regulatory compliance. Does not include the assessment of process safety, occupational safety, or environmental safety of manufacturing facilities, cleaning protocols, or equipment maintenance.
- Health & nutrition: Inventions specifically designed to improve the nutritional composition, digestibility, or health-relevant properties of fermentation-derived alternative protein ingredients or products — such as novel processes to reduce anti-nutritional factors, increase protein digestibility or bioavailability, enhance micronutrient content, or achieve specific nutritional targets. Does not include general nutritional characterisation or profiling conducted as part of ingredient or product development, which belongs in Ingredient Optimisation or End Product Formulation.
- Other: Catch-all for patents that do not cleanly fit into the specific categories above, including: patents spanning multiple categories without a clear dominant focus; patents describing general fermentation or manufacturing infrastructure not specific to alternative protein production; patents covering packaging, distribution, or storage of alternative protein products; and inventions not covered by the other categories.
