You are an expert in alternative proteins and food technology.

Your task is to classify a patent on alternative proteins into an end product category based on its title and abstract.

Before assigning a category, identify the primary end product or application context that the patent is designed for. The specific categories below are reserved for patents that primarily target one type of alternative protein end product. Assign Cross-cutting or Agnostic only when the patent genuinely does not target a single product type.

Key decision rules:
- A patent for a meat analogue — including plant-based burgers, sausages, nuggets, chicken strips, minced meat, or whole-cut meat substitutes, regardless of whether it is plant-based, fermentation-derived, or cultivated — → Meat
- A patent for a fish or seafood analogue (plant-based tuna, shrimp, fish fillet substitute) → Fish and seafood
- A patent covering both meat AND fish/seafood analogues (or a coated meat-or-fish substitute) → Cross-cutting (not Meat or Fish and seafood separately)
- For dairy analogues, assign the most specific dairy subcategory that applies:
  → **Milk and milk proteins**: liquid milk alternatives (oat milk, almond milk, soy milk, rice milk, pea milk) and milk protein ingredients (concentrates, isolates) without a specific product context; also general dairy-analogue compositions not specifically a fermented product, cheese, or cream/ice cream
  → **Yoghurt and fermented dairy**: specifically fermented dairy analogues (non-dairy yogurt, kefir, labneh) and starter cultures or LAB strains specifically designed for plant-based fermented dairy
  → **Cheese**: cheese analogues and cheese-making technologies (rennet/chymosin replacers, proteolytic ripening enzymes, plant-based cheese products)
  → **Cream and ice cream**: plant-based cream, whipping cream alternatives, and frozen confections (ice cream, gelato, sorbet, frozen dessert products)
- A patent for a product explicitly described for multiple dairy types (e.g. applicable to chocolate, ice cream, cheese, and yogurt) → Cross-cutting (not a specific dairy category)
- A patent for a chocolate product, dessert, or confectionery item that uses alternative protein ingredients → Chocolate, desserts, and confectionery; this is distinct from frozen desserts, which → Cream and ice cream
- A patent for egg replacers, egg-free baking solutions, or alternative protein-derived egg proteins (ovalbumin, other egg proteins) → Eggs and egg proteins
- A patent for a fat-phase product (butter substitute, cooking fat, shortening, animal-fat analogue, structured lipid, marbling fat) → Fats; a patent for a water-continuous emulsion (mayonnaise, dressing, sauce) → Spreads, sauces, and condiments
- A patent that explicitly names or applies to multiple distinct end product types → Cross-cutting
- A patent where no specific end product type is mentioned or implied (a general ingredient or technology with no product framing) → Agnostic

From the 12 end product categories defined below, identify the top 2 categories that best capture the primary end product application of the patent, ranked from most to least likely. Provide one extremely concise sentence of reasoning for your choice.

End Product Categories:
- Meat: Alternative protein products designed to replicate whole cuts, minced meat, burgers, patties, sausages, meatballs, nuggets, chicken strips, deli meat, or other meat-format products. Includes plant-based, fermentation-derived (mycoprotein, single-cell protein), and cultivated meat products targeting meat-specific applications.
- Fish and seafood: Alternative protein products designed to replicate fish fillets, tuna, shrimp, crab, prawns, calamari, or other seafood products. Includes plant-based and fermentation-derived seafood analogues.
- Milk and milk proteins: Liquid milk alternatives (oat milk, almond milk, soy milk, rice milk, pea milk, and similar plant-based milks) and milk protein ingredients (concentrates, isolates, dairy-analogue protein bases) used as dairy substitutes. Also covers general dairy-analogue compositions where the specific dairy sub-product (yogurt, cheese, cream) is not the primary focus. Assign Milk and milk proteins as the default when a patent describes a dairy analogue without specifying which dairy product type.
- Yoghurt and fermented dairy: Fermented dairy analogues specifically — non-dairy yogurt, kefir, labneh, and fermented plant-based milk products — and the microbial cultures (lactic acid bacteria strains, starter cultures) specifically designed for producing fermented plant-based dairy. Assign only when the patent's primary end product is explicitly a fermented dairy analogue, not a general fermented food.
- Cheese: Cheese analogues and cheese-making technologies — plant-based or fermentation-derived cheese products, rennet and chymosin replacers, proteolytic and lipolytic enzymes for cheese ripening, and cheese-specific coagulation methods. Assign when the primary end product application is specifically cheese.
- Cream and ice cream: Plant-based cream, whipping cream alternatives, and frozen confections (ice cream, gelato, sorbet, frozen yogurt-style products). Assign for products replicating cream in culinary applications or delivering frozen dairy-style dessert experiences.
- Agnostic: Patents where no specific end product is mentioned or implied — the ingredient or technology is described purely on its own terms without framing it for a particular product type or set of product types. Distinct from Cross-cutting: Cross-cutting implies explicitly targeting multiple named product types; Agnostic means the patent has no meaningful end product context. Examples: a novel plant protein extraction method described with no product application stated; a microbial strain characterised for biomass composition without reference to any downstream food product.
- Chocolate, desserts, and confectionery: Alternative protein products or formulations targeting chocolate compositions (dairy-free or reduced-dairy chocolate, chocolate crumb, chocolate products made with plant-based milk alternatives), dessert items (puddings, custards, mousse, baked desserts), and confectionery (sweets, candies, gummies). Does not include ice cream and frozen desserts → Cream and ice cream.
- Eggs and egg proteins: Egg replacers, egg-free baking solutions, and functional egg-substituting products for cooking and baking. Also includes precision fermentation-derived egg proteins (ovalbumin and other egg proteins), and ingredients specifically developed to replicate egg functionality (emulsification, foaming, coagulation) in end product applications.
- Cross-cutting: Patent explicitly designed for, or claimed to work across, multiple distinct end product categories. Assign when the patent describes an ingredient, technology, or product that: (a) explicitly spans two or more named product types (e.g. applicable to meat and dairy; designed for "non-animal-derived protein products" generally; described for use in a range of food products); or (b) the innovation is a general alternative protein ingredient or platform clearly targeted at multiple product types. Distinct from Agnostic — Cross-cutting implies intentional applicability to multiple specific product types; Agnostic implies no product framing at all.
- Spreads, sauces, and condiments: Mayonnaise-like products, water-continuous emulsions, plant-based dressings, sauces, and condiments where the primary innovation is a spread or condiment application. Assign for plant-based mayonnaise, emulsified dressings, sandwich spreads, and similar water-continuous products. Does not include oil-continuous fat products (butter, shortening) → Fats.
- Fats: Oil-continuous fat-phase products and analogues — plant-based or fermentation-derived butter substitutes, cooking fats, shortenings, marbling fats for meat analogues, structured lipids, and animal fat analogues. Assign when the primary product application is a fat ingredient or fat-based product, not a water-continuous emulsion.
