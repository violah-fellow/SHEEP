Prompt version 3 — changes and rationale
Based on test run review (round 2), 7 misclassified cases analysed.

---

1. CONFIDENCE SCALE RECALIBRATED (Cases 1, 3)
   Previous: level 3 = "probably out of scope but explicitly mentions AP or known AP ingredients"
   New:      level 3 = "probably out of scope, but the technology/ingredient is directly applicable
                        to AP product development even without a stated AP application"
             level 4 = "genuinely uncertain" (absorbs the old level-3 meaning as a sub-case)
   Rationale: LLM was assigning confidence 1-2 to patents covering general food technologies
   (e.g. colourants for food/beverage, structured fats for foodstuffs) that are clearly
   applicable to AP products. Viola wants these to score 3-4, not 1-2, to reflect implied
   relevance even when no AP application is stated.

2. BIOMASS FERMENTATION APPARATUS IN SCOPE (Case 2)
   Added new In Scope Boundary: apparatus and methods explicitly designed for biomass
   fermentation (not general fermentation for beverages, chemicals, pharma) are in scope
   under the fermentation pillar as enabling technologies, even without naming a specific
   organism or food application.
   Rationale: A container specifically described for "fermentation of biomass" implies
   bioprocess use for growing microbial biomass, a core AP production route. The LLM
   excluded it for lacking an explicit food/AP organism mention.

3. STARCH CROP PROTEIN MODIFICATION IN SCOPE (Case 5)
   Added new In Scope Boundary: patents on modification, processing, or functional
   improvement of protein isolated from starch/staple crops (corn protein, wheat gluten,
   rice protein) are in scope when protein is the primary subject and functional
   improvements concern food ingredient properties.
   Rationale: The starch crop exclusion targets general agronomic research on crops
   (yield, pest resistance). When a patent is specifically about improving isolated protein
   functionality, the crop exclusion should not apply. LLM excluded a corn protein
   deamidation patent because corn is listed as a starch crop.

4. MULTI-SOURCE FORMULATIONS AND GENERIC PROTEIN TERMS → CC (Cases 4, 6)
   Expanded the cross-cutting definition to cover:
   (a) Generic protein descriptors ("protein powder", "plant protein", "protein-rich
       material") in addition to "alternative protein" and "non-animal protein"
   (b) Formulation patents listing protein sources from multiple pillars as interchangeable
       or optional ingredients (e.g. "proteins from pulses, grains, or microorganisms")
   Rationale: Case 4 — a wet extrusion system using "protein powder" was labelled PB
   because of meat substitute framing, despite unspecified protein source. Case 6 — a
   vegan egg substitute listing proteins from pulses/grains/microorganisms was labelled PB
   despite microorganism proteins (fermentation-derived) being listed as an option.

5. BOOLEAN FLAG ASSIGNMENT GUIDANCE FOR CROSS-CUTTING (Case 7)
   Added a "Flag assignment guidance" section before the Output line.
   Key rule: when cross_cutting=True because the protein source is unspecified, set
   plant_based, fermentation, and cultivated all to False.
   Rationale: The LLM reasoned correctly (CC) for an animal-free dairy/egg substitute
   with cellulase but set fermentation=True (because cellulase is a fermentation enzyme)
   alongside cross_cutting=True. The post-processing code assigns F in this case because
   the CC derivation logic requires either >1 pillar flag OR (cross_cutting AND 0 pillar
   flags). Setting fermentation=True for the auxiliary enzyme broke the CC assignment.
   The flags should reflect the AP product's protein source, not auxiliary ingredients.
