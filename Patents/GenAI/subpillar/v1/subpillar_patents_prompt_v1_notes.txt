Subpillar (BF/PF/NA) prompt — v1 test run notes
Applied to: prompt_subpillar_patents_v1.md

v1 accuracy: 93% (n=60)
By category: BF 100% (30/30), PF 87% (26/30)
Confusion: of 30 PF ground-truth patents, 26 correct, 3 predicted NA, 1 predicted BF.

All 4 errors were PF ground truth misclassified as NA or BF — no BF patents were ever
wrongly predicted PF or NA, so the fix should focus entirely on tightening PF recall
without touching what's already working for BF.

---

1. NAMED PF TARGET MOLECULES DEFAULTING TO NA WHEN PRODUCTION ISN'T DESCRIBED

METHOD FOR PRODUCING CASEIN AND USES THEREOF (EP-4747269-A1) → LLM: NA; GT: PF
  Abstract only describes a heat-treatment step to increase casein ratio in an existing
  composition ("for producing cheese substitute") — no mention of fermentation, a host
  organism, or recombinant expression anywhere.
  Reasoning: "no mention of fermentation, microbial production, or recombinant expression
  of casein."

The LLM only assigns PF when the abstract itself narrates a production/expression step.
But casein, whey protein, egg proteins, collagen, and heme/myoglobin are specifically
called out in the prompt as canonical PF products (established recombinant targets in
this space) — when a patent names one of these as its subject, the fermentation-derived
route should be the default assumption even if the abstract only describes downstream
processing of it, since a fermentation-pillar patent about "casein" is very unlikely to
mean animal-extracted casein.

Fix: Added a rule that these named target molecules → PF by default, even without the
words "fermentation"/"recombinant" appearing and even when the abstract's focus is a
downstream processing step, unless the abstract explicitly states a natural animal/plant
source instead.

---

2. MIXED BF+PF PATENTS DEFAULTING TO NA INSTEAD OF THE MORE NOVEL PF CLAIM

COMPOSITIONS AND APPLICATIONS DERIVED FROM OGATAEA POLYMORPHA (AR-130716-A1) → LLM: NA; GT: PF
  Reasoning: "The patent describes both whole-cell biomass/extracts... used directly as
  food ingredients/flavor precursors (BF) and recombinant expression of specific
  hemoproteins (e.g., bovine myoglobin) purified from transgenic O. polymorpha (PF), with
  comparable emphasis on both routes across the claims."

The LLM correctly spotted both routes, but the v1 prompt's NA rule ("comparable emphasis,
neither clearly primary") fired as soon as both were present, rather than recognising that
transgenic/recombinant production of a specific named molecule is usually the more novel,
patent-worthy claim even when broader biomass/extract embodiments are also described.

Fix: Added an explicit tie-break — when a patent mixes biomass/extract use with
transgenic/recombinant/engineered production of a specific named molecule from the same
organism, prefer PF. Narrowed the "comparable emphasis → NA" rule so it only applies when
neither side involves engineered production of a named molecule (i.e. genuine dual-use
patents, not this pattern).

---

3. ACCEPTED CASES — NO PROMPT CHANGE (genuine information limits in the abstract)

- OGATAEA POLYMORPHA DERIVED COMPOSITIONS AND APPLICATIONS (US-20250134149-A1) → LLM: BF; GT: PF
  Same organism/family as case #2 above, but this abstract is much shorter and only says
  "the use of a methylotrophic yeast and its derivatives as a food additive and source of
  flavoring compounds" — no mention of the transgenic myoglobin/heme production present in
  the sibling family member's abstract. Given only this text, BF is the reasonable read;
  the ground truth here likely reflects knowledge from the fuller patent family that this
  abstract doesn't contain. Not fixable by prompt wording — the abstract itself doesn't
  carry the distinguishing signal. Flagging in case family-level context (e.g. family_id)
  becomes available to the pipeline later, since that would resolve this class of case
  directly rather than needing the prompt to guess.

- NOVEL FOOD COMPOSITION AND METHOD FOR PRODUCTION THEREOF (WO-2025078458-A3) → LLM: NA; GT: PF
  Describes enzymatic hydrolysis (glucanase/xylanase) of a lignocellulosic waste stream
  into a flour-like "upcycled material" — no organism, fermentation step, or specific
  named target molecule mentioned; glucanase/xylanase are named generically as tools, not
  as the patent's product. NA is the defensible read from the abstract alone. Did not add
  a general "any enzyme mention → PF" rule, since that would be over-broad and likely
  create false positives on genuine BF/bioprocess patents that also use enzymes
  incidentally (e.g. downstream processing steps in biomass patents).

---

Net effect of the two fixes: directly addresses 2 of the 4 v1 errors (EP-4747269-A1,
AR-130716-A1). The other 2 are information-limited rather than prompt-logic errors, so v2
should land around 28/30 PF (93%) rather than 30/30 — the remaining gap looks like a data/
pipeline question (patent family context) more than a prompt one.
