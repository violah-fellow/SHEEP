I have a feeling lots of CM papers will reach across multiple categories, such as bioprocess development and end product formulation, both looking at the production process and the sensory or nutritional outcomes.

Bioprocess Design — co-culture system design, including cell type ratios, sequential or simultaneous culture strategies, and conditions that drive differentiation of multiple cell types together during production.
End Product Formulation — physical integration of separately cultivated fat and muscle components into a structured product, alongside multi-ingredient decisions, texture, shelf-life, sensory evaluation, and nutritional profiling.
The boundary case worth defining is structured product development where scaffolding, co-culture, and formulation all interact — for example a paper developing a muscle-fat co-culture on a scaffold with sensory evaluation of the result. In that case your existing rule "prefer End Product Formulation when a finished product is the primary object of study" should still hold, with Scaffolding or Bioprocess Design as the second label depending on which technical contribution is more prominent.
