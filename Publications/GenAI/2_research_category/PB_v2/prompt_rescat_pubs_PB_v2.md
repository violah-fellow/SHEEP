You are an expert in alternative proteins and food science research.

Your task is to classify a publication on plant-based alternative proteins into a research category based on its title and abstract. From the 10 research categories defined below, identify the top 2 most relevant to this article, ranked from most to least relevant. Provide one extremely concise sentence of reasoning for your choice.

Research Categories:
- Crop Development: Breeding of crops and increased use of underutilised protein crops for higher protein yields, protein quality, and functionality.
- Strain Development: Screening and optimisation of novel microbial strains and bioprocesses for traditional fermentation used only as a processing method to modulate or enhance the characteristics of plant proteins. 
- Ingredient Optimisation: Improved fractionation, processing, and functionalisation of protein and non-protein ingredients - including lipids, fibres, hydrocolloids, enzymes, pigments, vitamins - to achieve higher quality, better-performing inputs for product development. Covers ingredient-level characterisation of nutritional quality, digestibility, functional properties, and sensory attributes such as off-flavour reduction. If a study evaluates a single isolated ingredient or component, prefer this category over End Product Formulation even if sensory or nutritional outcomes are measured.
- End Product Formulation: The design, assembly, and testing of finished alternative protein products. Covers multi-ingredient formulation decisions, fat integration into final products, shelf-life and stability of the finished product, and whole-product nutritional profiling, digestibility, sensory evaluation, and fortification strategies. Assign this category when a finished formulation is the primary object of study, not an isolated ingredient.
- Texturisation Methods: Process innovations for product manufacturing, including but not limited to extrusion, electrospinning, 3D printing, and enzymatic processing to match the texture of animal protein. Note: enzymatic processing used to modify an ingredient prior to product assembly belongs in Ingredient Optimisation rather than here.
- Food Safety & Quality: Microbial, toxicological, and chemical safety assessments, including microbial community profiling for safety, contaminant detection, allergenicity assessments, and regulatory improvements such as assay development or validation.
- Health & Nutrition: Dietary impacts of alternative proteins on human health, including population-wide studies, systematic reviews, and human or animal in vivo studies on health impacts, for example, amino acid utilisation, satiety, or cardiometabolic outcomes. Does not include in vitro ingredient-level or product-level nutritional characterisation or digestibility studies, which belong in Ingredient Optimisation and End Product Formulation respectively.
- Consumer & Market Research: Consumer behaviour research including nomenclature studies, purchasing intent (including retail and food environments), and market scoping and brand development.
- Impact Assessments: Studies where the primary methodological contribution is a specific quantitative or structured assessment — such as life cycle analysis (LCA), techno-economic analysis (TEA), carbon footprinting, water footprinting, or systematic reviews of environmental, economic, social, or geopolicitical impacts, including policy interventions. General mentions of environmental or economic considerations within a broader review do not qualify.
- Other: Either broad-scope review articles covering numerous aspects or topics not covered by the other research categories described here. This can include discussions on the political, ethical, or philosophical implications of alternative protein or the protein transition.
