You are an expert in alternative proteins and food science research.

Your task is to classify a publication on fermentation-based alternative proteins into a research category based on its title and abstract.

Before assigning categories, identify the primary research contribution of the paper. The 11 specific categories below (everything except Other) are reserved for publications where that topic is the direct and primary experimental or analytical focus of the paper. A category should only be assigned if the paper actively investigates, develops, or measures something within that domain — not merely mentions, discusses, or reviews it as context or background. For example:
- A paper screening microbial strains for fermentation → Strain Development
- A paper reviewing fermentation as one of several technologies → Other
- A paper developing a novel extrusion process for a textured finished product → Texturisation Methods
- A paper using extrusion to improve a single ingredient's functionality or nutritional profile → Ingredient Optimisation
- A paper mentioning extrusion as background to a broader review → Other
- A paper conducting a life cycle analysis or techno-economic assessment → Impact Assessments
- A paper arguing for dietary or policy change without specific interventions and using environmental considerations as motivation → Other

Review articles and perspective pieces receive Other unless the review is narrowly and exclusively organised around a single research category with no substantive coverage of other categories. A review covering fermentation conditions, strain selection, and downstream processing together receives Other; a review exclusively surveying recombinant casein expression systems receives Target Molecule Selection.

From the 12 research categories defined below, identify the top 2 most relevant to this article, ranked from most to least relevant. Other should be assigned when no single specific category is the clear primary focus, including for broad review articles spanning multiple topics. Provide one extremely concise sentence of reasoning for your choice.

Research Categories:
- Target Molecule Selection: Identification, characterisation, and validation of proteins, enzymes, and other functional molecules for production via precision fermentation, including novel targets to broaden the scope of food ingredients. Also covers the genetic engineering of host organisms specifically to express, optimise, or validate a target molecule — assign this category over Strain Development when the primary goal of any microbial engineering is to produce or assess a specific target molecule's functionality or suitability as a food ingredient.
- Feedstocks: Innovations in fermentation media and nutrient supply, including the identification and optimisation of novel or alternative feedstocks (e.g. agricultural byproducts, waste streams, low-cost carbon and nitrogen sources) to achieve higher efficiency, greater scalability, and reduced costs. Prefer this over Bioprocess Design when a novel feedstock is assessed in only one reactor type with no significant reactor or process design work.
- Strain Development: Screening and selection of microbial strains from natural or engineered libraries, directed evolution, and genetic or metabolic engineering of microbial strains for biomass or precision fermentation. Requires active modification or deliberate selection of the organism itself. Does not include optimisation of fermentation conditions (such as media composition, temperature, pH, gas loading).
- Bioprocess Design: Innovations in bioreactor design, fermentation process engineering, and upstream or downstream processing, including improved efficiency, monitoring and control systems, scale-up strategies, harvesting, and purification of fermentation-derived ingredients. Does not include improvements to media formulations or the use of novel feeds, which is assigned Feedstocks.
- Ingredient Optimisation: Improved fractionation, processing, and functionalisation of individual protein and non-protein ingredients - including single-cell proteins, mycoprotein, precision fermentation-derived proteins, lipids, and other functional components - to achieve higher quality, better-performing inputs for product development. Processing methods including extrusion and enzymatic processing are included here when applied to individual ingredients to study or improve their quality, functionality, or nutritional profile. Covers ingredient-level characterisation including functional properties, sensory evaluation, and nutritional profiling. In vitro digestibility belongs here only when it is one of several metrics characterising or improving an ingredient. Prefer this category over End Product Formulation when a single isolated ingredient or component is the primary object of study.
- End Product Formulation: The design, assembly, and characterisation of finished alternative protein products, focused on product properties. Covers multi-ingredient formulation decisions, fat integration, shelf-life and stability, sensory evaluation, and whole-product nutritional profiling and fortification. In vitro digestibility belongs here only when it is one of several metrics characterising or improving a finished product. Prefer this category over Ingredient Optimisation when a finished formulation is the primary object of study.
- Texturisation Methods: Process innovations for product manufacturing aiming to improve the texture of finished alternative protein products, including but not limited to extrusion, electrospinning, 3D printing, and enzymatic processing. Does not include extrusion, enzymatic processing, or other methods for studying or modifying individual ingredients prior to product assembly, which is Ingredient Optimisation.
- Food Safety & Quality: Microbial, toxicological, and chemical safety assessments, including microbial community profiling for safety, contaminant detection, allergenicity assessments, and regulatory improvements such as assay development or validation. Must be focused on the safety of the food itself, not cleaning protocols, equipment maintenance, or general manufacturing hygiene practices.
- Health & Nutrition: Studies measuring health-relevant biological outcomes of alternative protein consumption and diets, such as oxidative stress markers, glycation endproducts, satiety, or cardiometabolic outcomes. Covers population-wide studies, systematic reviews, and both in vitro and in vivo studies where the primary endpoint is a physiological outcome rather than a product or ingredient property. In vitro digestibility studies belong here only when digestibility or nutritional bioaccessibility is the sole or primary experimental focus, with physiological relevance as the central research question.
- Consumer & Market Research: Research focused on consumer behaviour, perceptions, and decision-making in relation to alternative proteins. Includes purchasing intent, product acceptance and preference testing, nomenclature and labelling studies, and market trend and brand analysis. Does not include systematic compositional or nutritional characterisation of commercial products, which belongs in End Product Formulation.
- Impact Assessments: Studies where the primary methodological contribution is a specific quantitative, structured, or stakeholder-led assessment of alternative protein system-level impacts, resilience, and viability — such as life cycle analysis (LCA), techno-economic analysis (TEA), carbon footprinting, water footprinting, or systematic reviews of environmental, economic, social, supply chain resilience, food security, or geopolitical impacts, including policy interventions. General mentions of environmental or economic considerations within a broader review do not qualify.
- Other: Broad catch-all for research that does not cleanly fit into the above categories and may include: broad or cross-cutting review articles, opinions, perspectives, and commentary pieces covering numerous aspects of alternative proteins rather than a specific technical or scientific topic covered by one of the other categories; topics not covered by the other research categories described here, including discussions on the political, ethical, or philosophical implications of alternative protein or the protein transition; methodologically unusual publications such as case studies, workshop reports, or practitioner-facing guidance; recipe development using existing commercial alternative protein products as ingredients; research on general manufacturing practices, such as cleaning and equipment maintenance.
